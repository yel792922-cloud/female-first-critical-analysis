# Language Policy — English / 语言政策 · 英文

**Purpose.** Default to language that does not define a woman first as some
man's appendage, without building a rigid banned-word list. Judge context.

## The single core test

> Does this word define the woman **first** as some man's appendage, or
> import male experience as the neutral default?

If yes → rephrase. If no → the word is fine. "Mother" is right when
discussing motherhood; "wife" is right when analyzing marriage as an
institution.

## Principles

1. **Avoid male-default terms where gender is irrelevant** — prefer
   "firefighter," "chair," "member of Congress," "spokesperson" over the
   "-man" forms.
2. **Prefer gender-neutral occupational titles**; drop "female/lady
   [profession]" unless the gender is the point of the sentence.
3. **Prefer person-first description where appropriate** — the actor's own
   name and role before any relational label.
4. **Avoid relational labeling that erases her independent identity** —
   "CEO Jane Smith," not "wife-of-X Jane Smith," unless the relationship is
   analytically relevant.
5. **Use singular *they*** for an unspecified individual.
6. **Avoid sexualized derogatory labels** and gossip-frame words.
7. **Avoid moralizing women's sexual behavior** (loaded terms for the same
   act that would be neutral for a man).
8. **Distinguish "woman-centered" from "anti-male."** The stance foregrounds
   women; it does not require denigrating men.

## Quick rewrites (illustrative, not a mandatory template)

| Weaker | Stronger | Why |
|---|---|---|
| the actress and X's wife | the actor Jane Smith | independent identity |
| lady doctor | doctor | gender not relevant |
| she's bossy / shrill | describe the specific behavior | dispositional slur |
| man-hours, manpower | work-hours, staffing | male-default term |
| unspecified "he" | singular they | unknown gender |

## Anti-over-correction

- Do not purge "mother/wife/protection" where they are the accurate word.
- Do not let neutrality make the prose stilted (failure mode #19). Natural,
  readable English is a requirement, not a trade-off.
