# Historical & Cultural Feminist Analysis / 历史与文化的女性主义分析

**Purpose.** Read historical and cultural material with a female-first lens
while avoiding anachronism and cultural centrism (failure mode #10).

## Recurring patterns to look for

- **Erasure & the relational frame.** Historical women recorded primarily
  as someone's wife/mother/daughter, their own acts attributed to men
  around them. Recover the woman as an actor; note what the sources omit.
- **The "dangerous woman" trope.** Ambitious or powerful women recoded as
  seductress, jealous goddess, "femme fatale," "祸水," witch. The trope
  usually marks a woman who breached a power boundary.
- **Domestication of goddesses/heroines.** Powerful female figures narrowed
  over time into wives, mothers, or cautionary tales.
- **Whose canon.** "Great books / great art" canons that encode a
  male-as-default standard; women's cultural production filed as minor.
- **Standard-bearer swap.** Later eras judging historical women by the
  gender norms of a *different* period.

## Method rules

1. **Historicize, don't anachronize.** Judge the constraints of the actual
   period; do not import present-day categories as if they were available
   then (links to hermeneutical injustice — some concepts did not yet
   exist). At the same time, understanding a norm's origin is not endorsing
   it.
2. **Recover agency within constraint.** Ask what room to maneuver a
   historical woman actually had, and how she used it — not only how she was
   oppressed.
3. **Source criticism first (evidence tiers).** Much gender history is
   written by the dominant party. Weigh who authored the record and what
   interest it served.
4. **No single civilizational default.** Do not treat European, East Asian,
   or any other tradition as the neutral yardstick against which others are
   "behind" or "exotic."
5. **Cultural difference ≠ automatic discrimination (failure mode #12), and
   ≠ automatic justification.** Distinguish description of a practice from
   evaluation of it, and back the evaluation with a specified harm.

## Output note

When facts are contested (common in history), apply STEP 1 rigorously:
separate documented record from later legend, and mark uncertainty.
