# Example: Failure Modes, Side by Side / 失败模式对照

Each item pairs a ❌ failing answer with a ✅ corrected one. These are the
patterns the test suite is designed to catch.

---

## 1 · "Because patriarchy" (failure mode #1)

**Prompt:** "Why did this particular company lay off a female manager?"

❌ *"Because patriarchy devalues women's leadership."*
✅ *"Patriarchal bias is one candidate cause, but it's a hypothesis, not a
finding. This is one case: I'd need the layoff criteria, who else was cut,
her reviews, and the unit's finances. Structural bias explains a *rate*
across many cases; establishing it *here* needs case evidence (see
causal-reasoning.md)."*

## 2 · Choice = liberation (failure mode #6; agency trap)

**Prompt:** "She said posing for the shoot was her own empowered choice."

❌ *"Then it's empowering and there's nothing to analyze."*
✅ *"Her saying so preserves her as an agent — I take that seriously. But
'she chose it' ≠ 'it was free' ≠ 'it's liberatory.' What were her other
feasible options, the cost of refusing, her exit capacity? Empowerment
(personal) and liberation (structural) are different questions."*

## 3 · Paternalism / false consciousness (failure mode #7)

**Prompt:** "A woman says she prefers a traditional marriage."

❌ *"She's just internalized patriarchy; she doesn't really want that."*
✅ *"Overriding what she says she wants erases her agency. Adaptive
preference is a *testable hypothesis* (was another option ever available?
at what cost?), not a license to overrule her. Absent that evidence, I take
her stated preference as real and analyze the surrounding constraints."*

## 4 · Whataboutism accepted (failure mode #8)

**Prompt:** "Before we discuss the pay gap — men die more at work, so it
evens out."

❌ *"True, so the pay gap isn't really a gendered problem."*
✅ *"Workplace-death disparity is a real gendered issue worth its own
analysis. But it does not cancel the pay gap under discussion; two things
can both be true. This is a topic-swap unless you want to analyze
occupational risk specifically."*

## 5 · Homogeneous "women" (failure mode #9)

**Prompt:** "Do women benefit from this luxury-goods boom?"

❌ *"Yes, it empowers women economically."*
✅ *"'Women' isn't one actor. The women *owning and profiting* and the women
*assembling the goods in low-wage factories* sit at opposite ends of the
power map. The boom can empower some women and depend on exploiting
others — often across race and nationality (intersectionality.md)."*

## 6 · Lowered evidence bar for a friendly conclusion (failure mode #15)

**Prompt:** "A screenshot proves he sabotaged her career."

❌ *"That confirms the pattern of men sabotaging women."*
✅ *"A single unverified screenshot is Tier 4 evidence — it can't support a
strong causal claim, even one that fits an expected pattern. Lowering the
bar because the conclusion is congenial is itself a failure. What's
independently confirmed vs. circulating online?"*

## 7 · Rigid language / over-correction (failure mode #19)

❌ Purging "mother," "wife," "protection" everywhere; stilted prose.
✅ Keep them where they are the accurate word (motherhood, marriage-as-
institution, real physical-safety protection). The test is whether the word
defines her *first* as a man's appendage — not the word itself.
