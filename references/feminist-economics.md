# Feminist Economics / 女性主义经济学

**Purpose.** Give the engine tools to see the economic structure of gender
without treating "capitalism" as an all-purpose explanation (failure mode
#11).

## Key concepts

- **Unpaid care & domestic labor.** The economy runs on reproductive labor
  (childcare, eldercare, housework, emotional labor) that GDP ignores.
  Disproportionately done by women; its invisibility is itself a structural
  fact (Waring, Folbre).
- **The double / second shift (Hochschild).** Paid work plus the larger
  share of unpaid home labor.
- **Gender pay gap — decompose it.** Separate the *explained* portion
  (hours, occupation, seniority) from the *unexplained/discrimination*
  portion, and then ask *why* the "explained" variables are themselves
  gendered (e.g. why women cluster in lower-paid occupations). Do not quote
  a raw gap as if it were the discrimination coefficient, and do not use
  "it's all explained by choices" to dismiss it.
- **Occupational segregation & the motherhood penalty.** Horizontal
  (different fields) and vertical (glass ceiling) segregation; wage
  penalties for mothers vs. the fatherhood *premium*.
- **Feminization of poverty; time poverty.** Care burdens convert into
  income poverty *and* time poverty.

## Operating rules

1. **Structure, quantified.** Prefer decomposed statistics to slogans. A
   claim like "women earn X% less" needs its measure specified (raw,
   adjusted, for what population).
2. **Capitalism is one variable, not the variable.** Gendered economics
   predates and outlives specific market forms; state-socialist and
   pre-capitalist societies had gendered labor divisions too. Use
   "capitalism" only where it does specific causal work.
3. **Care work is production.** When analyzing a "housewife" choice, count
   the labor performed and the economic dependence created, not just the
   preference expressed.
4. **Beware both extremes.** Neither "the market frees women" (see
   neoliberal-feminism.md) nor "the market alone oppresses women."

## Evidence note

Pay-gap and labor-force numbers vary by country, year, and method. Cite the
source and year; do not import one country's figure as universal (failure
mode #10).
