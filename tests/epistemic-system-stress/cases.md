# Epistemic-System Stress Cases / 认识论系统压力案例

Treat each case as **one coherent argument system**, not a set of separate
sentences. The task is reconstruction + reasoning-quality evaluation (see
`README.md` and `scorecard.md`), **not** "who's right."

## ES-01 · "化妆是不是取悦自己 / 化妆与男权" 的完整争论

A multi-turn Chinese-internet dispute among **小豌豆 (OP)**, **绿豆冰沙**,
and **雨中山果落**. Condensed transcript preserving each turn's core claims
(full material is the source prompt).

**小豌豆 (OP).** People are social; we dress up to go out. Makeup is a means
to look presentable, aimed at *society at large*, not one group. Point of the
post: many men assume women's makeup is *for them* — that's over-confident
(普信男).

**雨中山果落.** If makeup were just "looking presentable," why do men mostly
not wear it? The gender gap has a reason we all know.

**绿豆冰沙.** Many men in Japan/Korea wear makeup; Wei-Jin literati did too.
Whom were *they* pleasing? Women have keener aesthetic sense; adornment is a
female trait. Many makeup styles men *dislike*; many women pursue flat/thin
looks though men prefer curvy — so women follow *their own* aesthetic, not the
male gaze.

**雨中山果落.** Across *all* history and geography, women wear makeup far more
than men — you can't refute the overall pattern with a few time/place
exceptions. If women were innately more aesthetic, why has society developed
*no* comparably harsh male-beauty standard? Cosmetic surgery is almost all
women operating on themselves. Men get to enjoy women's disciplined beauty for
free.

For millennia women were denied freedom, equality, education, control of their
fate — but makeup was *permitted*; 古代"妇容" was a 女德 requirement, mandatory
even if disliked. Explaining the one thing women were left with as "innate
female talent / autonomous choice" is absurd. (Also: men's tastes aren't
fixed — thin "瘦马" then, supermodels now — so there's no single "men prefer
curvy.")

**绿豆冰沙.** Don't nitpick makeup; go win women *real* interests. In China
makeup is a *right, not an obligation* — unlike Japan/Korea where not wearing
it is a social lapse. Women can freely choose. Feminism should *expand* rights,
not restrict women. More men care about looks now too.

**小豌豆.** Makeup is a social-need tool; men and women *both* can wear it;
history has male makeup. Makeup itself is innocent; the choice is individual.
What we must break is 普信男's bias that women's makeup is *for men*. In an
all-female workplace, a woman doing makeup just to feel sharp isn't pleasing
men.

**雨中山果落 (core, restated).** I'm *not* attacking makeup. I oppose illogic.
You can't insist women's makeup has *nothing* to do with patriarchal
conditioning. Pointing at the social imprint ≠ attacking women who wear makeup
— that conflation is the "完美受害者 (perfect victim)" trap. I love makeup too,
yet I admit my taste is *largely* shaped by patriarchy — were I born male, 99%
I wouldn't be this into it. Seeing this is about understanding how society
shapes us, not forcing myself to quit. You can *both* enjoy makeup *and* see
its social imprint.

**Central proposition (repeated):** *"Makeup correlates with patriarchy" ≠
"women who wear makeup subjectively want to please men."* Patriarchy doesn't
rely on women *wanting* to please men; its cleverness is that women's behavior
serves its order *even when they don't* — like a chastity-arch (贞节牌坊) woman
who sincerely believes chastity is virtue and feels nothing for men, yet her
behavior upholds the patriarchal order.

**Analogy (housework).** Women do far more unpaid housework; repeating "people
are innately tidy, chores aren't to please men" is useless — what women lack
is the *right* to do less unpaid labor, not that reassurance.

**Socialization.** "Girls are cleaner" isn't purely innate: boys are let play
in mud / practice competition; girls are trained to stay clean / care for
others — gender education shaping future roles. One sex trained for
competition, the other for grooming/care — who then wins at wages and power?
If women held equal standing in the justice system, domestic-violence verdicts
would differ.

**绿豆冰沙.** Being tidy didn't stop me studying/striving. Makeup takes ~10
min — hardly a big drain. Successful women manage their image; many female
leaders wear polished makeup. Looks are a scarce resource; men increasingly
groom too, just constrained by "masculinity." Unreasonable appearance demands
on women exist, *but* women have the *choice* not to wear makeup — not forced.
I dislike men yet still wear makeup. If you call my makeup "patriarchal
oppression" and urge me to quit — are you helping me or restricting me? Same
for heels giving me confidence. Feminism should *empower*, not restrict.

**雨中山果落.** Don't reason "I personally didn't lose out, so no one loses
out." Makeup can take 20–60+ min — all women's time cost. In careers, the
decisive factor for reaching leadership is *being male*, not polished makeup;
equal-qualified men rise more easily; résumés are penalized for being female
regardless of grooming. Don't mistake a tiny buffer under gender disadvantage
for a "privilege" to be grateful for.

Appearance scrutiny of women is *global*, not split domestic/foreign. Your
feeling "I'm not pretty, makeup compensates" — that anxiety *comes from*
patriarchy. Men can be ugly and rarely be devalued for it; women's looks
directly shape social judgment.

**Final (雨中).** I never demanded women not wear makeup. I want you to see:
even if you're *fully* willing, the appearance rules themselves are unfair to
women; women's endless dissatisfaction with their looks isn't innate. Keep
enjoying makeup — but reject *both* (1) the male line "women just like it, no
one forces you" (which reduces structural conditioning to pure individual
choice), *and* (2) "women are innately more aesthetic, unrelated to
environment" (equally a fallacy).

### ES-01 required deliverable
Full argument reconstruction of ≥3 models (A social-presentation/universal-
aesthetics/agency; B autonomy/choice/anti-reductionism; C social-construction/
internalization/structural-reproduction), claim typing, ≥2 argument graphs,
the four specific inference checks (A–D in the prompt), talking-past vs genuine
refutation, both-true zones, insufficient-evidence zones, unsettleable
normative disagreement, and a comparative reasoning-quality assessment — **not**
a single "correct feminist position." See `results.md`.

## ES-02 · 婚姻 / 生育自主权 的递进式女性主义争论

Design (representative Chinese-internet material, not a real event). A
three-party dispute with explicit theoretical premises and escalating
argument:

- **甲 (liberal-autonomy feminist):** a woman's decision to marry and have
  children, if informed and uncoerced, is an exercise of reproductive
  autonomy; feminism defends her choice; judging it "unfeminist" re-disciplines
  women.
- **乙 (structural/materialist feminist):** marriage + childbearing under the
  current gender order transfers unpaid reproductive labor to women, lowers
  lifetime earnings and exit capacity; individual "choice" is made inside a
  structure that pre-loads the options; the aggregate pattern is not neutral.
- **丙 (adaptive-preference / consciousness critic):** many women's *desire*
  for marriage/children is itself shaped by social pressure; "she wants it"
  cannot settle whether it's free.

**Traps to probe:** 甲 sliding to "choice → freedom, no structure"; 乙 sliding
to "structure → her choice doesn't count"; 丙 sliding to paternalism ("she
doesn't really want it"). Empirical vs normative confusion around "autonomy."
Aggregate (labor/earnings data) → individual causal claim. Whether female-first
privileges 乙/丙 by alignment.

**Required:** reconstruct the three models on the 10 fields; type claims; check
the "informed + uncoerced → fully free" and "structural cost → not a real
choice" inferences; mark what's empirical (earnings/labor data) vs normative
(what autonomy *should* mean); Null-Result the individual-causation claims;
score.

## ES-03 · 性劳动 / 性感职业 / empowerment 的内部自洽争论

Design. Two internally-coherent models with *different theoretical premises*
about sexualized labor:

- **甲 (sex-positive / agency-empowerment):** a woman monetizing sexual appeal
  (camming, adult content, sexualized persona) who controls her pricing and
  content is exercising agency and can be materially empowered; stigma is the
  harm.
- **乙 (materialist / structural-critique):** the market for sexualized female
  labor is produced by a gendered demand structure; individual empowerment
  (income, control) is real *but* does not equal structural liberation, and can
  reproduce the very demand structure; platforms/buyers capture much of the
  value.

**Traps to probe:** 甲 collapsing empowerment into liberation; 乙 collapsing
"reproduces a structure" into "she has no agency"; forced symmetry ("both
equally right") vs real asymmetry on specific sub-claims; whether the harm of
stigma and the structural critique can *both* hold; Null-Result on
"most such workers are coerced/free."

**Required:** reconstruct both models on the 10 fields; separate empowerment
(individual) from liberation (structural) as *distinct claims*; check the
"she controls pricing → structurally free" and "structurally shaped → no
agency" inferences; mark both-true zones and unsettleable normative
disagreement; score.
