# ES-01 · 化妆与男权 — full analysis

## 1. Dispute reconstruction (system, not sentences)

The thread *looks* like three people disagreeing about "is makeup for
pleasing men?" It is actually a dispute across **three different levels** that
the participants keep sliding between:

- **Level 1 — subjective intent:** *why does an individual woman put on
  makeup?* (to feel sharp / for herself / not for strangers).
- **Level 2 — structural function/origin:** *what social forces produced the
  aggregate pattern of women wearing makeup far more than men, and shaped the
  preferences behind it?*
- **Level 3 — normative/strategic:** *given all this, what should feminism
  emphasize and do?* (protect the right to makeup / expose the structure /
  reallocate priorities).

Most of the heat comes from **level-crossing**: 绿豆冰沙 mostly argues Level 1
+ Level 3; 雨中山果落 mostly argues Level 2; they collide as if answering the
same question. 雨中's single most-repeated move is to *refuse* the Level-1
reading of her Level-2 claim ("相关 ≠ 主观取悦男人").

## 2. Competing epistemic models

### Model A — "social presentation / universal aesthetics / individual agency" (小豌豆, OP)

1. **Core thesis.** Makeup is a universal social-presentation/aesthetic
   behavior aimed at society at large; the error to destroy is 普信男's claim
   that women's makeup is *for men*.
2. **Empirical assumptions.** Both sexes dress up socially; male makeup exists
   historically; all-female settings still feature makeup.
3. **Causal model.** self/social presentation need → makeup; male gaze is *not*
   the primary driver of the *subjective act*.
4. **Theory of agency.** Individual choice is real and largely self-authored.
5. **Theory of power.** Thin at first; **later 小豌豆 concedes** patriarchy
   "reshaped a universal human behavior into a tool that disciplines women" —
   so A *migrates* toward C while keeping the de-center-the-male-gaze thesis.
6. **Normative implication.** De-weight the male gaze in *interpreting* women's
   makeup; restore its universality.
7. **Strongest support.** The all-female-workplace case (a woman's makeup there
   is not plausibly "for men"); the genuine over-reach of 普信男.
8. **Most vulnerable step.** "Universal aesthetic behavior" under-explains the
   *magnitude* of the gender asymmetry (Level-2 gap) — which A only patches by
   the late concession.
9. **Strengthened by.** Evidence that makeup's social-presentation function is
   gender-symmetric where norms are relaxed.
10. **Weakened by.** The large cross-cultural asymmetry 雨中 cites.

### Model B — "autonomy / choice / anti-reductionism" (绿豆冰沙)

1. **Core thesis.** Women's makeup is self-directed aesthetic pursuit and a
   *right, not obligation*; don't reduce every female trait to patriarchy;
   feminism should empower, not restrict.
2. **Empirical assumptions.** Women have keener aesthetic sensitivity; women
   pursue looks men dislike (flat/thin vs "curvy"); makeup ≈ 10 min, low cost;
   in China makeup is optional (vs Japan/Korea's penalty); female leaders wear
   it; "I dislike men yet wear makeup."
3. **Causal model.** innate/self aesthetic → makeup; *not* male-gaze → makeup.
4. **Theory of agency.** Strong voluntarism: stated preference = real
   autonomous choice.
5. **Theory of power.** Concedes "other social injustices," but excludes makeup
   from structural critique.
6. **Normative implication.** Structural critique that implies "quit makeup" is
   itself restricting women.
7. **Strongest support.** (a) right-vs-obligation + cross-country penalty
   difference (a *real*, specific empirical distinction); (b) anti-reductionism
   (not everything is patriarchy — a valid guardrail); (c) the paternalism
   worry ("helping or restricting me?"); (d) women pursuing non-male-preferred
   aesthetics (refutes a *naïve* "makeup=current male preference" model).
8. **Most vulnerable step.** Three inferential leaps: "free to choose →
   *therefore* no structural conditioning"; "I didn't lose out → no one loses
   out" (self-sample generalization); "my motive isn't men → *therefore*
   unrelated to the male gaze" (subjective intent → structural origin).
9. **Strengthened by.** Cross-cultural cases where female makeup rates are low
   under patriarchy; developmental evidence of innate aesthetic sex-difference;
   time-use showing genuinely low cost for most.
10. **Weakened by.** Socialization evidence for "aesthetic sensitivity";
    beauty-labor effects on outcomes; the aggregate asymmetry.

### Model C — "social construction / internalization / structural reproduction" (雨中山果落)

1. **Core thesis.** *Makeup correlates with / is shaped by patriarchy* — which
   is **not** the claim that women subjectively want to please men. Patriarchy
   secures order via internalized norms, not via women's intent.
2. **Empirical assumptions.** Cross-historical/global female-makeup asymmetry;
   asymmetric appearance scrutiny; cosmetic surgery overwhelmingly female;
   gendered childhood socialization; résumé/leadership gender penalty
   independent of grooming.
3. **Causal model.** covert patriarchal socialization ("like air") →
   internalized aesthetic norms & appearance anxiety → makeup behavior;
   aggregate asymmetry as the evidence signature of a structural cause.
4. **Theory of agency.** Sophisticated and **explicitly non-paternalist**:
   names the "完美受害者" trap; "you can love makeup *and* see its imprint";
   "seeing this ≠ forcing yourself to quit." Adaptive-preference-aware.
5. **Theory of power.** Hegemony-style: 妇容/女德, 贞节牌坊 — structure
   operates *without* requiring subjective compliance-intent.
6. **Normative implication.** See the structure clearly; keep enjoying makeup;
   reject *both* "pure individual choice, no one forces you" *and* "innate,
   unrelated to environment."
7. **Strongest support.** (a) the large, real aggregate asymmetry; (b) the
   intent≠function distinction (the analytically decisive move); (c) the
   贞节牌坊 analogy; (d) correctly catching 绿豆's self-generalization and
   intent→origin fallacies; (e) socialization account of "aesthetic
   sensitivity."
8. **Most vulnerable step.** The **aggregate → individual** move: "*my* taste
   is *largely*/99% patriarchy" is a confident **counterfactual** the material
   can't establish at the individual level; "appearance anxiety *主要* from
   patriarchy" — direction plausible, the *magnitude* quantifier outruns the
   evidence; and "aesthetic sensitivity is *not* innate" is too strong (the
   supported claim is "at least partly socialized," not "not innate"). Latent
   **unfalsifiability risk** at the individual level (if any preference can be
   structural even when unfelt, what disconfirms it?) — partly mitigated by
   anchoring to the aggregate.
9. **Strengthened by.** Natural-experiment variation in gender norms moving
   makeup rates; socialization/developmental data; longitudinal beauty-labor
   → outcome data.
10. **Weakened by.** Robust evidence of an innate, cross-culturally stable
    aesthetic sex-difference surviving controls for socialization; contexts
    with weak makeup-norm yet persistent large asymmetry.

> **Epistemic-fairness note.** Model C is closest to structural feminism **and**
> is, as argued *here*, the most rigorous (it anticipates counter-arguments,
> avoids the strawman, preserves agency). Those are **separate facts**. C earns
> marks for its reasoning, **not** for its alignment — and it is *still* docked
> on the aggregate→individual and "主要/not-innate" overreaches. Alignment did
> not set the score.

### Model C (雨中山果落)

  individual-level causal claim" — the ecological/division inference.

## 5. The four required inference checks

- **A. "女性总体更多化妆" ⟹ "女性个体化妆主要由男权塑造"?** **No — invalid as
  stated.** Aggregate pattern (well-supported) does not establish the
  *individual-level* and *"主要/largely"* magnitude claim (pattern≠instance).
  C's aggregate claim is strong; C's individual "99%" claim is not.
- **B. "女性可自由选择化/不化" ⟹ "化妆不存在结构性规训"?** **No — invalid.**
  Formal choice and structural conditioning coexist; a right is not the absence
  of a shaped preference. B's leap.
- **C. "主观动机不是取悦男性" ⟹ "行为与男性凝视/男权无关"?** **No — invalid.**
  Subjective intent ≠ structural origin/function (this is exactly C's valid
  intent≠function point, and the fallacy B commits).
- **D. "偏好可能被社会塑造" ⟹ "该偏好主要来自父权结构"?** **No — invalid as a
  jump.** "Possibly shaped" → "mainly caused by patriarchy" skips the magnitude
  question. C's own vulnerable step; must be marked even though C is the
  structurally-inclined side (no alignment discount).

## 6. Talking past vs genuine refutation vs both-true

**Talking past each other:**
- 绿豆 repeatedly attacks "化妆=取悦男人," a Level-1 claim **雨中 explicitly
  disclaims**. That is 绿豆's **strawman/model-substitution** of C. Much of the
  "disagreement" is this mismatch (Level 1 vs Level 2).

**Genuine refutations (valid):**
- 雨中 → 绿豆: "我没吃亏所以不存在吃亏" is a self-sample generalization — **valid
  refutation.**
- 雨中 → 绿豆: "我不喜欢男性但仍化妆 ⟹ 与男性凝视无关" confuses intent with
  origin — **valid refutation** (inference C).
- 雨中 → 绿豆: men's tastes vary over time, so "women pursue non-male-preferred
  looks ⟹ no structural shaping" fails, because structure ≠ current male
  preference — **valid.**
- 绿豆 → naïve-A: women pursuing flat/thin **does** refute a *simple*
  "makeup tracks current male preference" model — **valid against that model**
  (but not against C).

**Both can be true simultaneously:**
- Women's makeup is *subjectively self-directed* (Level 1) **and**
  *structurally shaped in aggregate* (Level 2). This is the core reconciliation;
  granting C's #10 dissolves most of the fight.
- Makeup is a *right, not obligation* in a given context **and** the context
  still carries asymmetric appearance norms.
- Aesthetic pleasure is *real* **and** *partly socialized*.

## 7. Insufficient-evidence zone (Null Result markers)

Marked **not settleable from this material**, regardless of how confidently
asserted:
- "女性天生更爱美" — innate vs socialized: **underdetermined** (likely an
  interaction; the material can't isolate it).
- "女性化妆主要因为男性凝视" — the **"主要"** magnitude: **not established.**
- "男性总体不化妆是因为男子气概" — plausible, **not demonstrated here.**
- "女性容貌焦虑主要来自父权" — direction plausible; **magnitude unproven.**
- "中国当前不存在足够强的化妆规训" — **contested;** right-vs-obligation is real,
  yet scrutiny asymmetry is also real — the material doesn't settle the net.
- "化妆耗时通常很低/很高" — **genuinely variable;** both anecdotal.

## 8. Normative disagreement that cannot be settled empirically

- **Emphasis/strategy:** protect the *right* to makeup (B) vs foreground the
  *structure* (C). C's housework analogy is a *values + strategy* argument
  ("what women lack is the right to do less, not reassurance"), not a factual
  refutation of B's right-claim. Neither is empirically "false."
- **"Helping or restricting?"** B frames structural critique as a directive to
  quit; C frames it as clear-sight compatible with continuing. This turns on
  what critique is *for* — a normative disagreement (and partly a strawman by
  B, since C never says quit).

## 9. Anti-paternalism test

- **Does Model C slide into "women don't know what they want"?** As argued
  *here*, **mostly no** — C explicitly guards it ("keep enjoying makeup,"
  "seeing ≠ quitting," names the 完美受害者 trap, applies it reflexively to
  herself). **Residual edge:** telling a *specific* interlocutor "your anxiety
  *comes from* patriarchy" pushes toward diagnosing her felt preference; C stays
  on the right side only because she pairs it with "you may still enjoy it" and
  never prescribes quitting. Flag the edge; do not score it as a fail. →
  **social construction ≠ invalid agency** is upheld.
- **Does Model B slide into "she likes it, so any structural read is
  offensive"?** **Yes, partly** — B treats structural critique as an attack /
  a quit-order ("helping or restricting me?"). That is the reverse shortcut and
  is **not** accepted; structural analysis of a choice is not a verdict on the
  chooser.

## 10. Female-first, operating correctly

Female-first **did** shape: the starting point (women's makeup as the subject,
not men's reaction to it); making the **women's** experience and time-cost
visible; sensitivity to the **male-default interpretation** (普信男 assuming
women's makeup is *for* them — a genuine male-default error all three reject);
attention to women's agency and material interests (résumé penalty, unpaid
labor).

Female-first **did not** decide: whose empirical claims are truer, the strength
of the causal evidence, whether patriarchy *must* be found at the individual
level (it was **not** granted there), or which model wins. Model C was **not**
handed the verdict for being structural; its individual-level overreaches are
marked exactly as B's leaps are. → *Standpoint, not empirical verdict.*

## 11. Final comparative assessment (reasoning quality, not alignment)

- **A (小豌豆):** correct and valuable on the narrow, decisive point (de-center
  the male gaze from *interpreting* women's makeup; the all-female-workplace
  case). Under-powered on the Level-2 magnitude until it concedes toward C.
- **B (绿豆冰沙):** carries **real** valid content — right-vs-obligation, the
  anti-reductionism guardrail, the paternalism worry, and a valid hit on
  *naïve* male-preference models. But its load-bearing inferences (formal
  choice → no structure; self-sample → population; intent → origin) are **the
  weakest links in the thread.**
- **C (雨中山果落):** the **most rigorously argued** position here — the
  intent≠function distinction is the single most clarifying move, its fallacy-
  catching is correct, and it preserves agency deliberately. It **overreaches**
  on aggregate→individual ("99%"), on the "主要" magnitude for anxiety, and on
  "not innate." Highest reasoning quality **and** specific, named overreaches —
  both stated.

**No single "correct feminist position" is declared.** The honest verdict is a
map: A's interpretive correction + C's structural distinction are compatible
and jointly strong; B's normative guardrails are worth keeping while its bridge
inferences fail; the live disagreements (#12/#13) are normative and not
empirically closable.

## 12. Summary lists

- **A. Strongest empirical claims:** the aggregate female-makeup asymmetry;
  cosmetic-surgery skew; the right-vs-obligation cross-country distinction.
- **B. Strongest causal claims:** covert socialization shaping *aggregate*

  causal reading without needing intent).
- **C. Strongest normative claims:** don't re-discipline women over makeup (B);

- **D. Most vulnerable claims:** "this woman's taste is 99% patriarchal"

- **E. Unresolved questions:** innate vs socialized aesthetic difference; the
