# Power Mapping / 权力地图

**Purpose.** A concrete inventory so "power" never stays an abstraction.
For any actor in the analysis, ask *how much of each resource they hold*
and *how they can convert one into another*.

## Thirteen resource axes (STEP 3)

1. **Economic** — income, assets, control over money, financial dependence.
2. **Information** — who knows what; access to facts, records, networks.
3. **Institutional** — formal authority granted by an org, state, or law.
4. **Media** — ability to shape the public account of events.
5. **Legal** — standing, protection, ability to sue or be shielded.
6. **Organizational position** — rank, hiring/firing, gatekeeping.
7. **Bodily & reproductive** — control over one's body, reproductive labor
   and its costs.
8. **Time** — discretionary time vs. time captured by others' needs.
9. **Emotional labor** — who is expected to manage others' feelings.
10. **Discursive authority** — whose framing is treated as the default.
11. **Platform power** — reach; ability to mobilize an audience or mob.
12. **Social reputation** — credibility that survives an accusation.
13. **Exit capacity** — the ability to leave without catastrophic cost.

## How to use it

- **Map, don't assume.** Fill the axes from evidence. A person can be
  subordinated on some axes and dominant on others — this is the normal
  case, not an exception.
- **Watch convertibility.** Money buys legal power; platform buys reputation;
  institutional position buys discursive authority. Power compounds across
  axes; that is often where the real story is.
- **Exit capacity is decisive for agency.** Low exit capacity turns a
  formal "choice" into a constrained one (see agency reference).
- **Same gender ≠ same power.** A woman CEO and a woman line worker sit at
  opposite ends of most axes. Gender does not erase the class/position gap;
  the map keeps you honest about intra-gender power.

## Anti-patterns

- Declaring "she has no power" without checking platform, reputation, or
  legal axes.
- Declaring "he has all the power" without checking the specific relation.
- Treating gender as the *only* axis when class, race, or nationality
  dominate the actual outcome.
