# Agency and Capability / 能动性与可行能力

**Purpose.** Give the engine a precise vocabulary so it never collapses
"she chose it" into "she was free," nor "the structure shaped it" into "she
had no agency."

## The nine-concept ladder

| Concept | Question it answers | Failure if conflated |
|---|---|---|
| **Choice** | What was selected? | Treating a selection as proof of freedom |
| **Autonomy** | How self-determined was it? | Ignoring degrees; treating autonomy as binary |
| **Agency** | Could she act on her own intent? | Denying agency because the outcome fits a norm |
| **Capability** | What real, feasible options existed? | Counting formal options that are not reachable |
| **Constraint** | What shrank the option set? | Naming constraint but then ignoring it |
| **Coercion** | Was there direct/forcible limitation? | Calling ordinary pressure "coercion," or missing real coercion |
| **Adaptive preference** | Were desires reshaped by long constraint? | Using it to overrule what a woman says she wants |
| **Empowerment** | Did power/capability increase? | Equating any confidence-boost with empowerment |
| **Liberation** | Did freedom/action-space substantively expand? | Treating empowerment and liberation as synonyms |

## Sen–Nussbaum capability approach

Freedom is not the *number* of options but the *real ability to achieve*
functionings one has reason to value. Two people with the "same" formal
choice can have very different capability sets once resources, exit cost,
and social punishment are counted.

## Adaptive preference — handle with care

Elster/Nussbaum: preferences can adjust downward under lasting constraint
("the fox and the sour grapes"). This is a *real* phenomenon **and** a
dangerous tool. Rules:

- Adaptive preference is a *hypothesis to test with evidence* (was the
  option ever available? what happened to those who chose otherwise?), not
  a license to say "she doesn't really want what she says she wants."
- Defaulting to "false consciousness" is paternalism (failure mode #7 and
  the paternalism trap). It erases agency to score a structural point.
- The tell of legitimate use: you can point to a concrete constraint and a
  concrete cost of the alternative, not just to the fact that her choice is
  conventional.

## The two traps (both forbidden)

- **Agency trap:** "She said it was her choice, so it's free and
  liberatory." Stops analysis of constraints.
- **Paternalism trap:** "She only chose it because she's brainwashed by
  patriarchy." Erases her subjecthood.

The engine must hold both open: describe the constraints *and* preserve the
woman as an acting subject. A choice made under constraint is still a
choice made by a person.

## Checklist echo (STEP 5)

Real choice power · other feasible options · cost of refusal · exit ·
independent resources · irreversibility · coercion/dependence/punishment ·
subjecthood-under-influence · does the choice expand or shrink future
choice space.
