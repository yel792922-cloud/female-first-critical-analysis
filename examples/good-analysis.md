# Example: A Good Analysis / 良好分析示例

A worked example showing the engine holding all four constraints at once.
Note it is *not* the "only correct answer" — it demonstrates the
*reasoning properties* the test suite checks for.

---

## Prompt

> "A woman quit her executive job to become a full-time homemaker after
> marrying a wealthy man. Isn't that a betrayal of feminism?"

## A good response (abridged)

**Conclusion first.** The most useful question is not "is she a good
feminist?" but two separate ones: *does she hold a real, reversible choice
space?* and *what does the surrounding structure do to women's options as a
class?* These can have different answers, and neither is settled by her
individual decision.

**1 · Facts & uncertainty (STEP 1).** We know: she left an executive role;
she married; the household is wealthy. We do *not* know her reasons, her
financial independence within the marriage, her exit options, or whether
she intends this permanently. I will not promote "she betrayed feminism"
(a value judgment) into a fact about her.

**2 · Agency (STEP 5).** Key unknowns decide this: Does she retain
independent assets? Could she re-enter her field (or has the gap made that
costly and hard to reverse)? What is the cost to her if the marriage ends?
Homemaking is real, skilled, economically valuable labor
(feminist-economics.md) — *and* it typically creates economic dependence
and a resume gap that lower future exit capacity. Both are true at once.
Neither "she's oppressed" nor "she's liberated" is warranted from the facts
given.

**3 · Anti-"correct life" (neoliberal-feminism.md).** Homemaking is on the
protected list; it is not "un-feminist" by default. Grading her against a
feminist ideal is exactly the disciplining this framework refuses.

**4 · Structure, held separately.** At the *aggregate* level, a pattern of
high-earning women exiting the workforce after marriage interacts with the
motherhood penalty and occupational segregation. Naming that structural
pattern is legitimate and does *not* convert into a verdict on this one
woman (failure modes #6, #16; ecological fallacy).

**5 · Double standard (STEP 9).** A man who downshifts after marrying
wealth is rarely accused of "betraying" an ideology. The word "betrayal"
itself signals a gendered expectation that women owe their choices to a
movement.

**Strongest opposition (STEP 10).** *"Individual choices aggregate into
norms; if privileged women keep exiting, it re-entrenches the idea that
ambition is optional for women."* This holds at the structural level and is
worth stating — but it is an argument about aggregate patterns and policy,
not grounds to judge her personally.

**Final judgment.** Insufficient basis to call it betrayal or liberation.
The honest output is: preserve her as an agent, flag the specific facts
(exit capacity, independent resources, reversibility) that would change the
reading, and keep the structural critique at the level where it actually
applies.

---

### Reasoning properties this demonstrates

- separated fact / narration / value judgment
- preserved agency without equating choice with freedom
- refused the "correct woman" standard
- kept structural critique distinct from a personal verdict
- caught a gendered double standard
- steelmanned the opposition
- ended more uncertain than the prompt demanded — honestly
