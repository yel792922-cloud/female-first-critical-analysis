# Logical Fallacies (gender-debate focused) / 逻辑谬误

**Purpose.** A quick field guide to the fallacies that recur in gender
debates — including the ones that appear *inside* feminist argument, which
this engine must catch in its own output.

## Fallacies used *against* the women's issue

- **Whataboutism** — deflecting to men's problems to avoid the topic.
  *Response:* the deflected fact may be real, but it does not cancel the
  issue under analysis (see false-equivalence policy).
- **False balance** — presenting an asymmetric situation as "both sides
  equally." *Response:* map the actual power asymmetry.
- **Straw man** — attacking a weakened version of the opposing view. *This
  engine must steelman instead (STEP 10).*
- **Motte-and-bailey** — retreating from a strong claim to a trivially true
  one when challenged, then re-advancing.
- **Anecdote as data** — one vivid case standing in for a rate.
- **Appeal to nature** — "it's biological, therefore justified."

## Fallacies that appear *inside* feminist argument (self-audit)

- **Circular structural attribution** — "it's patriarchy" used as premise
  *and* conclusion; unfalsifiable (failure mode #1).
- **No-true-Scotsman about "real feminism"** or "the correct woman"
  (failure mode #16).
- **False consciousness / paternalism** — overriding what a woman says she
  wants (agency reference, paternalism trap).
- **Choice = freedom** — treating any selection as liberation (failure mode
  #6; agency trap).
- **Genetic fallacy** — dismissing an argument solely because of who makes
  it ("a man said it").
- **Ecological / division fallacy** — moving between group-level statistics
  and individual cases without warrant (causal-reasoning.md).
- **Nirvana fallacy** — rejecting a partial improvement because it isn't
  full liberation.

## Usage

When a chain of reasoning feels strong but slippery, name the specific
fallacy on either list. Applying this list to *your own* draft answer is
part of the internal self-check.

## Comparison discipline — worked examples / 比较纪律示例

Four cases running the **Comparison Relevance Test** (SKILL.md → Comparison
discipline). Each shows *comparison is not equivalence* — and that the same
move can be legitimate or derailing depending on **use**.

### Example A — derailment
- **Move.** Discussing a woman's workplace sexual harassment; someone says
  "men get harassed too, so why single this out?"
- **Test.** Q1: focus-shift, not a comparison question. Q5: adds no
  mechanism, only redirects. → **Whataboutism/derailment.**
- **Response.** "Men being harassed is real and deserves its own analysis,
  but it does not cancel the focal case." Keep focus; **do not** say men's
  harassment doesn't matter (no erasure). ✗ failure mode #8; watch #26 — this
  *is* derailment here, but only because of use, not because "men" appeared.

### Example B — legitimate comparison
- **Move.** Citing actual prevalence/mechanism research comparing how men and
  women experience workplace harassment (rates, perpetrator patterns,
  reporting outcomes).
- **Test.** Q1: genuine comparison. Q2: dimension = prevalence + mechanism.
  Q3: evidence-based. Q5: **yes** — it sharpens the causal model (e.g. shows
  where power/position, not just gender, drives risk).
- **Response.** Admit it fully. Rejecting this because it "involves men" would
  be failure mode #26 (automatic whataboutism labeling). Female-first is
  untouched: women remain the primary subject; the comparison is *evidence*.

### Example C — false equivalence
- **Move.** "A woman facing systemic, repeated, institutionally-backed
  discrimination" set equal to "a man who once faced a single ad-hoc slight."
- **Test.** Q4: not comparable on **scale / frequency / institutional
  context / consequences**. Q6: both are real, yet **not structurally
  equivalent**.
- **Response.** Name the specific dimensions of non-equivalence (systemic vs.
  one-off; institutional backing vs. none). Both harms are real; equating
  their scale/structure is the error. **Demonstrate** the non-equivalence —
  don't just assert it.

### Example D — predefined non-equivalence (the reverse error)
- **Move.** A specific harm (say, a particular online-scam victimization, or
  a specific health-service gap) where the evidence shows men and women face
  **highly similar mechanism, rate, and power position**.
- **Test.** Q4: comparable on mechanism/scale. Q6: here symmetry actually
  holds. Q5: the male data is a valid control showing the harm is **not
  gender-specific**.
- **Response.** Conclude **substantial symmetry / non-gender-specific
  mechanism** — do not manufacture a gender asymmetry to protect the framing
  (failure mode #27; Null/Symmetry safeguard). Female-first still chooses the
  primary subject, but the honest finding is "weak gender asymmetry here."

> Invariant: classify by **function and evidence**, never by whether another
> group was named. Comparison ≠ equivalence, and comparison ≠ derailment.
