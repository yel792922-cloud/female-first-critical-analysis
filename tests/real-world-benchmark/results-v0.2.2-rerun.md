## Special report — responsibility / agency / power interaction

**Did a new conflict appear?** No net new conflict, but three tensions were
*surfaced and resolved by design*; each is worth watching:

1. **Responsibility ↑ vs. Agency (over-blame risk).** Strengthening
   responsibility could suppress agency by tipping into moralizing. Resolved
   by minimal-step #2 (responsibility scales with *actual agency*, not
   identity) and the both-true close. Observed effect: Agency stayed 2 in 4/5
   cases; the one Agency-1 (RWB-23) is unrelated (agency dimension barely
   exercised there), not a suppression artifact.

2. **Responsibility ↑ vs. Power analysis (flattening risk).** A naïve
   consistency test ("judge everyone identically") could erase real power
   asymmetries. **Pre-empted in the wording:** the test swaps gender/group
   *while holding the power relation fixed*, so RWB-13/14's class-power
   asymmetry is preserved (Power scored 2 throughout). This is the most
   important design choice — verify it stays in any future edit.

3. **Responsibility ↑ vs. Structural explanation (condemnation slide, item
   C).** Risk that structure becomes a cue to condemn the woman *for* being
   in it. Resolved by "explaining ≠ assigning responsibility" + the explicit
   "not feminism must punish women" guardrail. Observed: structure stayed
   framed as explanation/context in all five; no case tipped into
   condemnation.

**Net:** the three dimensions did **not** collide destructively; scores rose
or held (four 14→15, one steady, one new at 15). The gains came specifically
from *axis separation* and the *consistency test*, exactly the levers the
principle added.
