# Feminist Epistemology / 女性主义认识论

**Purpose.** How the female-first stance changes *what counts as knowledge*
without lowering the bar for *whether a claim is true*.

## Core ideas

- **Situated knowledge (Haraway).** Every claim is made from somewhere. The
  "view from nowhere" is usually a view from the dominant position dressed
  up as neutral. Naming the standpoint improves objectivity; it does not
  replace evidence.
- **Standpoint theory (Harding, Hartsock, Collins).** People in
  subordinated positions can see things the dominant miss. This is a
  *hypothesis-generating* advantage, not a guarantee of correctness. A
  standpoint tells you where to look; it does not certify what you find.
- **Strong objectivity (Harding).** Include the analyst's own social
  location in the analysis. Bias hidden as neutrality is worse than bias
  named.
- **Epistemic injustice (Fricker).** *Testimonial injustice*: a speaker's
  credibility is deflated because of who they are (e.g. a woman reporting
  harassment). *Hermeneutical injustice*: a group lacks the shared concepts
  to name its own experience (e.g. before "sexual harassment" was a term).

## Operating rules for this skill

1. **Female-first is a lens, not a verdict.** Prioritizing women as
   subjects tells you *which questions to ask first*. It never pre-decides
   the answer.
2. **Credibility asymmetry cuts both ways.** Correct for the reflex that
   discounts a woman's testimony — *and* do not inflate any testimony to
   fact. A woman's account is evidence of her experience, not automatically
   proof of external causation.
3. **Name the standpoint, keep the evidence bar.** "This is analyzed from a
   female-centered standpoint" is a disclosure, not a discount on rigor.
4. **Distinguish experiential authority from causal authority.** A person
   is the authority on *what happened to her*; she is not automatically the
   authority on *why, at a structural level*, it happened.

## Common misuse to avoid

- Treating standpoint as "lived experience overrides data." It does not.
- Treating "situated" as "therefore unfalsifiable." Situated claims are
  still testable.
- Using epistemic injustice to shut down any disagreement as "silencing."
  Disagreement backed by evidence is not testimonial injustice.
