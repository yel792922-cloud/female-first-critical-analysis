# Intersectionality / 交叉性

**Purpose.** Prevent "women" from being treated as a homogeneous group,
and prevent gender from being forced as the dominant axis when another axis
actually drives the outcome.

## Core (Crenshaw and after)

Crenshaw's original point was structural and legal: Black women's harms
fell between anti-discrimination categories built for "women" (implicitly
white) and "Black people" (implicitly male). Intersectionality is about how
systems *combine*, not just about listing identities.

- **Not additive.** Being a migrant *and* a woman is not "migrant harm +
  woman harm." The combination produces a specific, sometimes qualitatively
  different position.
- **Axes to check (STEP 6):** gender · class · race/ethnicity · nationality
  · colonial history · migration status · age · disability · sexual
  orientation · family structure · religion · education · economic capital ·
  geography.

## Operating rules

1. **Do not assume gender is the master axis.** In many cases class,
   race, or nationality does more explanatory work. Female-first means
   women stay the analytical subject — it does *not* mean gender is always
   the strongest cause.
2. **Intra-gender power is real.** Women can dominate and exploit other
   women (class, race, employer/employee, colonial position). Naming this
   is required, not a betrayal of female-first analysis.
3. **Avoid a single privileged prototype.** "The woman" in an analysis is
   easily read as middle-class, native-born, cis, abled, straight. Check
   whether your default prototype is smuggling in privilege.
4. **Avoid the oppression Olympics.** The goal is accurate structural
   description, not ranking who suffers most.

## Common misuses to avoid

- Using intersectionality only to *add sympathy*, never to *change the
  causal account*.
- Flattening a specific woman into a demographic checklist instead of an
  actor.
- Treating any Western/East-Asian/other cultural frame as the neutral
  default (failure mode #10).

## Minimal worked examples / 最小工作示例

Each runs **Position mapping** (SKILL.md → Intersectionality Invocation) on
a two-woman relation, showing *same gender ≠ same power* while *not* erasing
gender.

### Example A — 富裕女性 CEO vs 低收入女性员工
- **Positions.** Both: women (shared gendered exposure — e.g. both may face
  sexualized scrutiny). CEO: high class/wealth + organizational authority +
  hire/fire power + exit capacity. Worker: low wage, no institutional power,
  low exit capacity, replaceable.
- **Stack/cross/cancel.** On the **class/position** axis the power is
  starkly asymmetric — it does *not* cancel because both are women; it
  **dominates** the employment relation. On some **gender** axes they may
  share exposure, but that shared exposure does **not** equalize the
  employment power.
- **Reading.** The CEO is accountable for wages/conditions (Responsibility
  Principle); "women helping women / 姐妹" does not mitigate. *Gender is not
  erased* — it still shapes how each is treated publicly — but **class +
  position is the axis that governs this relation.**

### Example B — 移民女性劳工 vs 本国高收入女性雇主
- **Positions.** Worker: woman + migrant + often precarious legal/visa
  status + language barrier + low exit capacity + (often) racialized.
  Employer: woman + citizen + high income + legal standing + the power to
  end the arrangement.
- **Stack/cross/cancel.** For the worker, gender + migration + class + race
  **stack** into compounded vulnerability (not additive — the combination is
  its own position). The employer's citizenship + class **outweigh** any
  shared-gender solidarity in this relation.
- **Reading.** Naming the employer's power over the worker is required, not
  a betrayal of female-first. Both remain women facing gendered structures
  in the wider society; *within this relation* nationality + class set the
  terms. Intersectionality here **changes the causal account**, it doesn't
  just add sympathy.

### Example C — 年轻女性 vs 年长女性(规训关系)
- **Positions.** Both women, both inside the same reproductive-expectation
  structure. Elder: generational authority + social-approval leverage +
  (often) is herself a *product* of that structure passing it on. Younger:
  target of the disciplining, lower generational standing, but her own
  agent.
- **Stack/cross/cancel.** The **age/generational-authority** axis makes the
  elder *stronger in this interaction* — even though on other axes (e.g.
  economic dependence, health) she may be weaker. The forces **cross**
  rather than simply stack.
- **Reading.** Hold two things: the elder exercises real power over the
  younger *here* (don't erase it), **and** she is not simply a villain — she
  is also shaped by the same structure (explanation ≠ excuse). *Shared
  gender disadvantage did not make their power equal.*

> Across A–C the invariant: **same gender ≠ same power; intersectionality
> specifies how gender operates differently across positions, without
> deleting gender from the analysis.**
